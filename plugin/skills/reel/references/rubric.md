# Scoring rubric

Score each still and frame 1–10 on four criteria. A criterion's score for the round is its lowest
across the stills and frames, not the average: one bad frame is in the reel.

An 8 is work you would post under your own name. Most first drafts score 5–6 somewhere; a round 1
with every score at 8 or more means you looked at the frames you meant, not the ones you made.
Look again at the frames 0.1 s after each cut. A score only goes up when the frame changed.

## Composition

- **4**: elements collide, crowd an edge or sit under Instagram's buttons; nothing leads the eye.
- **6**: one focal point, but loose alignment, uneven margins, a corner empty by accident.
- **8**: one focal point; everything on a grid with deliberate margins; scale contrast between
  the bold idea and the rest; nothing in the safe zones.
- **10**: a frame you would print as a poster: every element placed, the space as considered as
  the things in it.

## Legibility

- **4**: text overlaps something, is cut off, is under 36 px, or leaves before it can be read.
- **6**: readable, but weak contrast, tight leading, a line broken mid-phrase, or two things
  asking to be read at once.
- **8**: reads at a glance on a phone at arm's length: big, high contrast, one thing to read at a
  time, lines broken where the sense breaks.
- **10**: the type is the design: the hierarchy is instant and the words land on the beat.

## Motion intent

Judge it from the frames around each cut and from the timeline.

- **4**: motion for its own sake, everything fades in, or a frame mid-transition shows overlap,
  mud or a half-gone element that looks broken.
- **6**: on the beat, but generic: the same slide-up everywhere; a transition reads as a jump
  cut rather than one thing becoming another.
- **8**: every move says something (a reveal, a change of state, an emphasis); springs suit the
  weight of what moves; frames at + 0.1 s and + 0.3 s look composed.
- **10**: the motion carries the idea on its own; you would know the subject from the movement
  alone.

## Brand fit

With no site or brand in the brief, judge fit to the brief: its subject, mood and words.

- **4**: could be about anything; colours and type unrelated to the subject; an invented claim.
- **6**: the palette or the type comes from the subject, but the copy is generic.
- **8**: the subject's own palette, type and words, and its logo where it has one, used with
  restraint.
- **10**: it looks like the subject's own team made it.
